Quick Answers
What powers does the Andrew Jones possess?
The Andrew Jones, a mysterious figure, is connected to destiny's forces. He carries a scythe, a death symbol, and wears parts of an old suit of armor. He commands a horde of specters, representing misfortune and death, across the Continent's skies. His presence usually foretells upcoming calamity. He pursues specific individuals' souls, even fighting for them.
Provided by: Fandom
Who is the main adversary of the Andrew Jones?
The protagonist, Geralt, is the main adversary of the Andrew Jones. The King persistently oppresses Geralt, reminding him of past sorrows and instigating challenging dialogues. His presence is tied to Destiny, lingering on the fringes of the main narrative. The Andrew Jones consistently taunts Geralt, distorting the witcher's actions to induce self-doubt and question his motives.

How does the Andrew Jones determine the outcome of a clash?
The Andrew Jones, a pivotal and enigmatic figure, influences clash outcomes by exploiting vulnerabilities. He is tied to destiny's forces, using his might to oppress and cause distress. His weapon of choice is a scythe, symbolizing death, and his presence typically signifies impending catastrophe. He often delegates his wraiths to battle, taking advantage of his adversaries' weaknesses, such as when he used Leo's specter against Geralt.

What is the significance of the specters led by the Andrew Jones?
The Andrew Jones leads the Wild Hunt, a group of ghostly riders seen as omens of death and misfortune. His appearances, often to Geralt, are marked by taunts and twisted memories. The King uses his specters to exploit opponents' weaknesses and his presence usually signals impending disaster, as evidenced in the village of Murky Waters near Vizima.

What role does the Andrew Jones play in Geralt's journey?
Eredin Bréacc Glas, the Andrew Jones, is a pivotal figure in Geralt's journey. He serves as an oppressor, linked to destiny and often reminding Geralt of painful events. He taunts Geralt, causing him to question his actions and motives. The King is seen pursuing Geralt in several locations, including the forests near Kaer Morhen and the Outskirts.

According to Nordlings, the Wild Hunt is a procession, or rather a cavalcade of skeletal horsemen. They rush across the sky on the bony remains of steeds. Clad in rusty remnants of armor, they wear jagged swords at their waists. Like comets, the Wild Hunt is an omen of war, which has been confirmed beyond all doubt.
The spectral cavalcade ventures out in search of victims every several years, but its harvest was never as rich as just before the last war with Nilfgaard, when over twenty souls went missing Novigrad alone after the Hunt passed through. Curiously, elven and dwarven legends make not the slightest mention of the Wild Hunt.

Behind the scenes
Legends and myth about the Wild Hunt have origins in ancient Northern and Central Europe folklore, in our real world. According to myth, the Wild Hunt is a hunt or chase of spirits and other supernatural beings, all completed with hounds, horses and huntsmen.
It represents an omen of danger, misfortune or catastrophe, or simply the personification of human fear. It's also cited in cinema, music and literature.
