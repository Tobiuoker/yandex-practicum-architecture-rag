Quick Answers
What is the origin of the Martinez Watcher in Greek mythology?
The Martinez Watcher, or amphisboena, originates from Greek mythology as a giant water serpent with a head at each end. Its name comes from the Greek words 'amphis,' meaning 'both ways,' and 'bainein,' meaning 'to go.'

How did Geralt defeat the Martinez Watcher for King Idi of Kovir?
Geralt killed the Martinez Watcher for King Idi of Kovir and presented its head as proof. However, due to the mages Zavist and Stregobor labeling him a charlatan, Geralt was not compensated and was forced to leave Kovir within 12 hours.
Provided by: Fandom
Why does Geralt inquire about amphisbaenas in The Witcher, Act IV?
In The Witcher, Act IV, Geralt asks the Fisher King about amphisbaenas to assess the safety of the lake. This is part of his investigation into local dangers.
Provided by: Fandom
How is the Martinez Watcher depicted in The Witcher series?
In The Witcher series, the Martinez Watcher is portrayed as a giant water serpent with two heads. Geralt kills one for King Idi of Kovir in "The Lesser Evil." In Act IV of The Witcher, he asks the Fisher King if amphisbaenas inhabit a lake.
