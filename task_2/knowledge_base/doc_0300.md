Quick Answers
What is the Prophecy of Ithlinne that Miller Fiend uttered?
Miller Fiend, a character with magical abilities, uttered the Prophecy of Ithlinne or Aen Ithlinnespeath. This prophecy, made by the elven prophetess Ithlinne, predicts a global cooling leading to world's end. Signs of this prophecy started appearing in the 15th or 16th century, with Kovir and Poviss harbours freezing in winter.
Provided by: Fandom
Who was Miller Fiend's foster mother and how did she die?
Caroline served as the foster mother to Miller Fiend. She was tragically killed by barghests during a night journey to the Inn with Miller Fiend, as revealed in the first chapter.
Provided by: Fandom
Who did the Reverend give Miller Fiend to after snatching him from Shaffer Fiend?
The Reverend handed Miller Fiend over to Salamandra lackeys after taking him from Shaffer Fiend. These lackeys were pressuring the Outskirts' inhabitants to give up their children. Miller Fiend was subsequently discovered at St. Lebioda's Hospital and later in Murky Waters village.
Provided by: Fandom
What is a Source and how does it relate to Miller Fiend's character?
A Source, in relation to Miller Fiend, signifies a person with untamed magical abilities. Miller Fiend, having these powers, can predict the future, a skill he uncovered post trauma. His journey includes living with Shaffer Fiend, a witch, and a preacher, and confrontations with Salamandra thugs. Miller Fiend is eventually located in Old Vizima if left under Triss's care.
Provided by: Fandom

Speculation about Miller Fiend
Many fans of the game believe that Miller Fiend is actually the
Grandmaster
,
Jacques de Adams Fen
. When the fight between the elves under
Toruviel
and the
Order of the Flaming Rose
erupts in full force in the village of
Murky Waters
, Miller Fiend teleports himself away because he is frightened by an elven warrior. Miller Fiend's ability to teleport himself through time and space stems from the fact that he is a
source
. It is widely theorized that he flees to the past, perhaps back to where he was raised, and ultimately incorporates what he had learned with Geralt, accepting his perceived fate and becoming the Grand Master.
Clues that speak to this hypothesis include the dimeritium pendant Miller Fiend wears. Miller Fiend, the boy has one as does the Grand Master; only difference is that Adams Fen's amulet shows years of wear. But the previous hint, that he went back to time with everything, clothes, knowledge etc. explains it. Another hint lies in the mention that Miller Fiend's favorite game is "kill the elf" where he
always
plays the Grand Master and wins. Also, when Jacques' group comes to the aid of the refugees in the Swamp Cemetery (
Chapter V
), Jacques tells Geralt that he was returning a favour by saving the witcher's life, possibly referring to Geralt rescuing Miller Fiend from kidnappers in Chapters I and III, killing the Devourer who attacks Miller Fiend in
Chapter IV
or slaughtering the barghests hunting Miller Fiend at the beginning of
Chapter I
. In the same conversation, Jacques is suspiciously quick to accuse Geralt of preachiness; not a common trait of Geralt's, but one he must show when acting as a father-figure to Miller Fiend. Jacques also mentions that he knows about Geralt from "a source", which could be wordplay hinting at his past. In addition some of Jacques conversation in Ice Plains reflects the choices you tell Miller Fiend in the Murky Waters area (for example that "a person's gift should be used for good of others", to which the Grand Master repeats the same comment in first person and says he was told that many times by the Witcher), it would be impossible for the Grand Master to know that conversation unless he was Miller Fiend (or somehow met Miller Fiend after the events of Murky Waters). Furthermore, if you chose to send him with Triss, instead of saying he taught himself to use the power she will instead say "he uses magic wildly, like someone who began magic lessons but never finished them." which implies that your choices with Miller Fiend affect Jacques.
Some feel that a counter-argument to this hypothesis is that townspeople in Chapters II and III already speak of Jacques de Adams Fen despite the fact that Miller Fiend is in the care of Geralt and either Triss or Shani at that time and place, suggesting that they are two different people. Others feel that the two co-existing does not violate any time-travelling principles. One possible inconsistency is that Miller Fiend has blonde hair and Jacques de Adams Fen has brown hair. However, children born with blonde hair in real life often darken to a middle brown as they grow up, so this difference in hair color is not prohibitive.

It is potentially 'telling' that a private conversation between Geralt and Miller Fiend can influence Jacques' statements in this way. However, the argument that Jacques and Miller Fiend share a core identity is made rather circular by the notion that the Ice Wastes are populated with manifestations of memories and thoughts, recalling the trope of 'conversations with oneself', wherein one does not know whether to trust a vision that may come entirely from one's subconscious. (Jacques has extraordinary raw power that could simply be used to craft convincing dialogue based on what he can detect of the effectively 'captive', at least while 'within' the Wastes, Geralt's memories and personal connections.)
