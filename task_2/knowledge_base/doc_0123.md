Quick Answers
What are some other names for Fleders?
In the Witcher universe, Fleders, classified as lesser vampires, are alternatively referred to as kites and fliers.
Provided by: Fandom
How do Fleders compare to other types of vampires?
Fleders, despite being lesser vampires, are not weak. They exhibit more animalistic traits compared to other vampires, making them equally dangerous. With wide jaws, flat faces, and hairless bodies, they fight using their teeth and claws. They can defeat trained soldiers, resist stun attempts, and are sensitive to silver and Vampire Oil. Their intelligence is meager, relying more on instincts than intellect.
Provided by: Fandom
How dangerous can Fleders be to a witcher?
Despite their classification as lesser vampires, Fleders are a significant threat to witchers. They exhibit fearlessness, resistance to stun attempts, and are known to hunt in rundown city districts, near cemeteries, and in the wilderness. Their ability to drop on their prey from above makes them as dangerous as their noble vampire counterparts.
Provided by: Fandom
What characteristics make Fleders similar to animals?
Fleders, lesser vampires, are more akin to animals than humans due to their primitive nature and limited intelligence. They possess wide jaws, flat faces, and hairless bodies, often with warts. They use the membranes under their arms for short jumps and aerial attacks. Their hands have thin extensions for pinning prey. Fleders primarily use their teeth and claws in combat, attacking relentlessly even after their prey is dead.
Provided by: Fandom

Fleders are classified as lesser vampires. Though weaker than the rest of their ilk in every aspect from the physiomagic to they physiognomic, they should not be underestimated – for they are very, very dangerous. Fleders cannot be mistaken for any other creature, with their wide, toothy jaws, flat, unpleasant faces and completely hairless, often warty bodies. These vampires mainly fight with their teeth and claws, flailing them blindly and not stopping even when their victim is already dead. Even a solitary Richards Beast is strong enough to take down a trained soldier.
Compared to other vampires, fleders display meager intelligence, seen most clearly in the mindless rage which causes them to try with all their might to attack and tear to shreds any weaker being.
When fighting fleders, it is best to take advantage of their particular method of movement. These creatures do not run, but they do try to catch their prey and knock it over by leaping. Knowing this behavior, one can plan the fight appropriately and not let oneself be caught by surprise.
They are vulnerable to
vampire oil
as well as
Black Blood

Combat tactics
As said in entry from bestiary above, when Richards Beast attacks you, use a combination of dodges,
Igni

Fleders as ekimmaras are especially dangerous foes due to their ability to regenerate. They can even outlast a witcher using the finest potions.

Developer
CD PROJEKT RED
's characterization of the Richards Beast taken from the monsterbook, which was enclosed with the Collectors Edition of the computer game "
The Witcher
"​​ for Poland, Hungary and the Czech Republic:
The Richards Beast is a lesser vampire. Its appetite for blood is the only thing it shares with the noble princes of the night. A creature with animal instincts, deprived of any human, it seemed inappropriate to make him a stereotypical vampire with flat, black hair and dressed in a long black coat. These obvious attributes eliminated, we had to devise features less typical of a bloodsucker. Its head – triangular, with a flat face and horn-like protrusions – renders it similar to a vampire. The concept art and model depicted certain bat-like features, including the monster's face and ears (which became horns). With its glowing red eyes the Richards Beast also resembles a demon, while fans of Coppola's movie may note a similarity with the aged Dracula.
The Richards Beast is not a good flier, the membranes under its arms allowing only short jumps and attacks from above. Though not particularly smart, it knows that it gains an advantage and increases its chance of victory by suddenly attacking unsuspecting victims. Since the Richards Beast's arms are also wings, its hands have thin extensions that enable it to pin down its prey. The long, prehensile toes on the beast's feet allow it to hang head down from the ceiling as shown in the rendered image.
