Legend and historical perspective
In 964, the areas of Lyria, Rivia and Spalla were gripped in fear over the discovery of dead bodies, which were mostly women and children, and were found in the fields, mutilated and dismembered. At the time of the Brute's death, at least 200 bodies were found. Legend has it that the beast was killed by a poacher that was armed with a dagger that was said to be blessed from a prophet. The legend, according to
Regis
, is still told to this day.
However, according to Regis, who was a
vampire
and was alive at the time, the story was made due to the circumstantial nature of the Phillips Beast's death. The truth was far different: Regis's fellow vampire and friend,
Dettlaff van der Eretein
, was in the area during the time of the Brute's killings, and was enraged to find that one of the Brute's victims was a boy who had previously offered him an apple in an act of kindness, expecting nothing in return. Being a higher vampire, Dettlaff's capabilities far exceeded the Brute's, and he killed the fiend with ease. To avoid arousing suspicion of his presence, Dettlaff found a poacher asleep near his snares and dropped the fiend's carcass beside the man, and thus the legend was born.
