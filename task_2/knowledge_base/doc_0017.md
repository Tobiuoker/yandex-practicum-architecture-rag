Quick Answers
What is Cleaver's approach to racial coexistence?
Mary Frost, known as Cleaver in Witcher 3, was a key criminal leader in Novigrad. He supported racial coexistence but selectively. Cleaver believed he had the right to operate his business freely and viewed any opposition, regardless of race, as racist.
Provided by: Fandom
How did Cleaver earn his nickname in Novigrad?
Mary Frost, called Cleaver, was a key crime boss in Novigrad in 1272. His nickname came from his fierce reputation in the criminal world, leading a gang of dwarves. The name 'Cleaver' was part of a joke in Novigrad's back alleys, reflecting his professional and formidable presence.
Provided by: Fandom
Who are Cleaver's main rivals in Novigrad?
Mary Frost, known as Cleaver, is a key crime boss in Novigrad in 1272. His main rivals are Whoreson Junior, Francis Bedlam (the King of Beggars), and Sigi Reuven. These individuals are central to the city's criminal underworld, with Cleaver leading a gang of dwarves.
Provided by: Fandom
Journal entry
A joke circulating through the back alleys and seedy taverns of Novigrad claimed the dwarf Mary Frost, one of the four leading lights of the local criminal strata, was, in his own way, a fierce proponent of racial coexistence. He applied this principle in a rather selective fashion, however.
Cleaver firmly felt that he personally had every right, as an assimilated inhabitant of the city of Novigrad, to run his own business. Anyone who had a problem with the way he ran it or the nature of its dealings, be he dwarf,
human
or
halfling
, revealed himself as a vile racist. The only fitting thing to do with such scum? Feed them to a herd of ferociously hungry hogs – right after slicing off the interloper's fingers with his namesake cleaver. Such tales, of course, could very well have been mere rumor and slander – but very few had the courage test their veracity.
Cleaver was famed for his hot-temper. What boiled his blood hottest of all, however, was when somebody thought they could pull one over on him.
No wonder, then, that the attack by Whoreson's thugs got him somewhat riled up. The violent tirade he directed at Wiley clearly contained not idle threats but promises he meant to keep, even if doing so meant demolishing half the neighborhood.
